# Prove Value: profitability plan
2026-09-29. Companion to the governing moat decision.

> The public record earns trust. Professional access, monitoring and
> infrastructure around that record generate revenue.

## What we never monetize

- The public record itself. Adjudications, evidence, correction chains stay
  inspectable; paywalling truth kills the trust thesis.
- The score as the product. The score is a view generated from the
  evidence layers, not the company.
- Ads. Dead for this niche.

## Revenue layers, in order

### 1. AI data companies (first money)
They buy structured datasets habitually; no category education needed.
Sell: bulk API, historical snapshots, embeddings, commercial licensing of
the promise graph. This is the cleanest near-term revenue because the buyer
already has a budget line for data.

### 2. Journalists and researchers (citations, not revenue)
Give the 10 canonical records away. They cite pv: objects in articles and
papers, which builds the citation layer that makes every later sale easier.
Cost: zero. Return: the distribution moat.

### 3. Institutional diligence (the $5K pilot)
For funds, research firms, diligence desks. A 30-60 day pilot:
- API access to 10 excellent promise graphs (promises, lineage, evidence,
  state history, challenges, stable IDs)
- One written accountability dossier on a project of their choice
- Live change feed on the 10 projects during the pilot
- Historical snapshots ("what was known and assessed as of June 2024")

Pitch: "Give your research agents structured, cited historical commitments
for every protocol you cover, so they stop reconstructing claims from the
open web." It replaces weeks of analyst time assembling the same record
badly. One paid pilot proves both the citation thesis and the revenue
thesis at once.

### 4. Forecaster markets (later, optional)
Take rate on prediction markets over promise outcomes. An option, not the
foundation. Only when a real crowd exists.

## Sequencing

Depth on the head first: 10 genuinely excellent records, then citations +
AI data sales, then fund pilots. The long tail becomes the fortress after
revenue, not before. Do not build the corpus for 18 months hoping demand
appears.

## Pricing logic

Price against the alternative: weeks of analyst time per dossier, or an
internal team maintaining the same infrastructure. Tiers separate casual
use (free/cheap public API, canonical IDs) from industrial use (rate
limits, bulk, feeds, snapshots, SLA, support, watchlists).

## The two empirical milestones

1. Someone outside Prove Value cites a pv: object as the authoritative
   record for a specific promise.
2. Someone pays $5,000 for a pilot.

Everything else is theory. Compliments are not validation; only these two
events change the thesis.

## Explicitly not doing

- Elaborate reputation economics before demand is proven (challenges start
  simple: submit -> editor reviews -> evidence recorded -> resolution
  published; reputation tracked invisibly).
- Tokenization, forecasting markets, or generalized AI agents pre-revenue.
- Selling "better crypto research" into the retail/journo/academic
  segments that love the product and won't pay for it.
